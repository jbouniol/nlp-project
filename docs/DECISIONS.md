# Design decisions

A short log of the choices behind the classifier: what we chose, the number that motivated it, and
the course reference. Newest entries at the bottom. Every number comes from a file in `results/`
or from the exploration notebook.

| Date | Decision | Evidence | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-17 | Data: `coastalcph/lex_glue`, config `ledgar`, pinned to Hub commit `c23fdff1` (2024-01-04). The three official splits are used as they are (train 60 000 / validation 10 000 / test 10 000): no re-split, no shuffle. | The validation split already exists; re-splitting would make our numbers incomparable with LexGLUE. | S01 p12 ("every decision taken on the test set makes the test score optimistic") |
| 2026-10-17 | Decision metric: **macro-F1** on validation; accuracy (= micro-F1) reported next to it. | 100 classes, very imbalanced (largest / smallest class ≈ 138× on train). | S01 p14, S04 p22, TP01 Ex 11 |
| 2026-10-17 | The test split is read once per final model, by the last "predict & save" cell of each training notebook and by the evaluation notebook; every choice (hyperparameters, epoch, `MAX_LENGTH`, LoRA config, class weights) is made on validation. | — | S01 p12, S06 p22 |
| 2026-10-17 | English encoder (`distilbert-base-uncased` as main model, one `bert-base-uncased` reference run); CamemBERT is not used. | LEDGAR clauses come from SEC filings, in English; CamemBERT is pre-trained on French. | TP03 "What we see", S05 p22 |
| 2026-10-17 | Environment pinned to the course versions (torch 2.14.1, transformers 5.19.0, datasets 5.1.0, peft 0.21.2, scikit-learn 1.9.1). The same `requirements.txt` resolves on the Mac (Python 3.14) and on Linux / Python 3.12 (Colab with CUDA, CI with CPU torch). | `uv pip compile` for both platforms. | — |
| 2026-10-17 | Macro-F1 is the mean of per-type F1 over the types that have at least one true clause in the evaluated split (`ledgar.metrics`). | *Books* has 0 validation clauses (23 train, 2 test): validation macro-F1 is always a mean over the same 99 types, whatever a model predicts. On the test split the 100 types are present and the score equals scikit-learn's `f1_score(average="macro")`. | S01 p14 |
| 2026-10-17 | `max_length = 512` for DistilBERT, with `TrainingArguments(train_sampling_strategy="group_by_length")`. Fallback if a T4 runs out of memory: batch 16 with `gradient_accumulation_steps=2`. | Training clauses kept whole: 58.9 % at 128, 86.5 % at 256, 95.1 % at 384, 98.2 % at 512. Tokens per epoch relative to 256 with random batches: 1.77 at 512 with random batches, 0.565 at 512 with length-grouped batches (0.497 at 256 grouped). `reports/data_summary.json`. Decided by the team on these numbers. | TP06 Ex 4, S06 p10, S05 p9 |
| 2026-10-17 | `max_words = 476` for the LSTM. | p99 of training clause length in words (median 86). Packing means the LSTM never pays for padding. | S04 p6, p10 |
| 2026-10-17 | The official test split is kept as it is, although 282 test clauses (2.8 %) are exact copies of a training clause; the final evaluation reports scores separately on clauses seen / not seen in training (`data.seen_in_train`). | Copies are contract boilerplate, not a broken split; 6 texts carry two labels, always close types (Waivers / No Waivers, Severability / Enforceability, Applicable / Governing Laws). | S01 p12 |
| 2026-10-17 | Per-band analysis by training count: tail < 300 clauses (22 types), middle, head ≥ 1 000 (16 types). Types with fewer than 10 test clauses (Books, Assigns, Qualifications) are always shown with their support. | Train counts: quartiles 312 / 427 / 667; only 3 types below 100, too few for a band of their own. | S04 p27 |
| 2026-10-17 | Lazy floor (always *Governing Laws*) saved as the run `results/lazy`, first row of the results table. | Validation: accuracy 0.049, macro-F1 0.001. | TP01 Ex 11 |
| 2026-10-17 | `FAST_RUN=1`: per-class subset (≤ 30 training, ≤ 5 evaluation clauses per type), 1 epoch, `max_length` 128, 200 bootstrap resamples, outputs in `.fast/` (not versioned). | A uniform sample of 2 000 clauses would lose most rare types and break per-class F1. | TP04, TP06 |
| 2026-10-17 | The package never reads the test split; a notebook reads it only in its final "predict & save" cell. Enforced by `tests/test_hygiene.py`. The exploration notebook also reads test **labels** for the class counts per split and the exact-duplicate check, never the lengths or the vocabulary. | — | S01 p12, S04 p5 |
